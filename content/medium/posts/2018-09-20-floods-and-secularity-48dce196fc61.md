<figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/725/0*0x7BeqXYm45Z_LWh.jpg" /></figure><p>The most basic duty of any government is to let the people of their country know what the basic principles are by which the nation should progress forward, what is the intrinsic value of being Indian. Been reading very interesting reasons for why one of the most devastating floods in independent India happened and to be very honest it’s a threat to the principles we thought lay foundation to our Indian identity. The last flood of this scale happened in 1924, interestingly the birth year of Bharatiya Janata Partys first prime minister to the country. The evening when the whole nation went into a seven day mourning for Vajpayee was when the situation was escalating to its worst phase. Loss to private property, public infrastructure and other damages scaling well above multiple thousands of crores and over 300 lives lost, the scale of the disaster was insanely high. Once the crisis situation was over and when people could start wasting their time saying opinions that never helps anyone positively, Bharatiya Janata Partys logic devoid unit of social media interns wanted to prove to the nation that they are better than the meteorological departments of this country in figuring out the causes of Natural Disasters. Allowing women entry into Sabarimala, eating beef and having a ‘lot’ of Christians and Muslims in the state are the supposedly ‘real’ reasons for the flooding. The last one is what I laughed the most at, let’s start there.</p><p>We all grow up studying civics and political science text books that have a brighter colour to highlight the word secularism. In 1947, post partition India was pioneered towards this beautiful word constitutionally. A country where your religious identity is never a reason for segregation, brotherhood and love based on humanity and yes, we had an example to show the world Kashmir. A Muslim majority state in a Hindu Majority country was beyond ideal. We all know where that story ended, India’s largest military cheque is cashed in the name of secularity today. Well, Kerala is unique in how it follows this term, the Gods own country has never had one god in its identity. Onam, the harvest festival of Kerala even after drawing its mythological roots from Hindu mythology has never been a Hindu festival, it’s always been the state festival. You could see Christians, Muslims, Jews, Sikhs and basically everyone celebrate it with the same amount (or sometime more) of enthusiasm as the Hindu Population of the state. Kerala is one of the best places to celebrate Christmas, every house would atleast have a star hanging regardless of the religion you follow. For malayalis it’s Jesus Christs Birthday Party not a Christian day of importance. People go crazy to host Iftaars and attend Iftaars throughout the holy month. The world’s only surviving Jewish Synagogue is in Kochi, The Edapally St George’s Forane Church has a Shiva pond inside, which is part of the Ganapathy temple behind. During Eid this year, when the state was still recovering from the flood and mosques flooded, numerous temples and churches opened its doors for Muslims to offer Eid namaz.</p><p>Flood relief in Kerala had a very unique element to it, even before central and state governments could get on to decide how to tackle the issue the citizens of the state were out helping each other. Religious centres took in people in the hundreds without even trying to know what their religion was and relief camps became home for everyone. The Sangh parivar and the RSS actively tried to make a dent on this situation, directing their volunteers to only help Hindu and trying to know the religion of the recipient before distributing aid. They were met with harsh critique and were mostly thrown out. The state held together, there were situations when relief camps in mosques directed their supply to temples nearby as the need there was direr. The North Indian hate towards Muslims and Christians largely originates from them being conquerors and not belonging to this land. Christians in Kerala are mostly Hindus who converted into Christianity when St. Thomas came to India, Muslims unlike their north Indian counterparts were not conquerors, and rather they came as traders. Sure, the harmony has a plethora of reasons which are not common to the rest of the country but why do you want to destroy that harmony when it exists. If having a lot of Muslims and Christians can cause floods, the Middle East wouldn’t be a desert. Secularity is what defined India as a country at birth seeing that as the reason to a natural disaster is a scary road to go down.</p><p>Granting women entry to Sabarimala is yet another reason to the flood. The debate is not new, Sabarimala has always denied women above the age of 10 and below the age of 50 entry into the temple. The legal battle against this has been going on forever and in a nation where you are not supposed to be discriminated based on your gender the arguments are fair. Interestingly Sabarimala is open to followers of all religions and has Vavar, a Muslim saint who migrated from Arabia to India with the intention of spreading Islam as an integral part of the pilgrimage. The problem with granting women entry to Sabarimala originate from the nature of the pilgrimage. Sabarimala is situated in the Western Ghat mountain ranges where the shrine is about a 3 hour trek from the base. Reaching the base is also a strenuous task in itself which includes travelling through mountains and forests. Given temples across the country deny women entry during menstruation and 10–50 being the age spectrum for the menstrual cycle, the denial of entry years ago did make logical sense when you had to travel nearly a whole month to complete the pilgrimage. With changing times and human progress, this rule largely seemed redundant and discriminatory. The repeal of this rule has gotten closer to reality and this is the second most interesting cause for the flood.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/771/0*VdapvDuqBy-Kf8MP" /></figure><p>When you’re a Hindu majority state that actively eats and promotes beef, the ‘Gau Raksha Dal’ blaming it as the reason for flooding is something we did see coming. When the nation was actively banning beef, Kerala had a beef festival every day. The basic civil liberty to consume what you want wouldn’t result in a natural disaster.</p><p>If anything, the cause for the floods are the Temples of modern India. The 33 dams that were opened as a result of heavy rains resulting in dams reaching maximum capacity was the singular reason for the floods to happen. We are not new to natural disasters but the right wing trying to portray it as a cause of the multiple things the affected state has questioned the centre on, is something we are new to. India has always been steered towards a country that tries to incorporate the multiple diversities it is home to and a federal democracy that gives every state the liberty to decide its policy but are we still driving in that direction? Probably not. Do the new principles that guide the nation read ‘hate everyone who do not follow the BJP’? Kerala showed the world that you can be your own god if you can lend a helping hand without asking the other person who his god is. It showed the reality of humanity. As the state works towards rebuilding what was lost with very limited support from the central government, we should introspect if this is the country we want to build.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/0*YjI7AEzQx7Yp4S1g" /></figure><p><em>Originally published at </em><a href="http://www.makriman.me/floods-and-secularity/"><em>www.makriman.me</em></a><em> on September 20, 2018.</em></p><img src="https://medium.com/_/stat?event=post.clientViewed&referrerSource=full_rss&postId=48dce196fc61" width="1" height="1" alt="">
