<p>12.05.2019, The national capital of Delhi went to polls for the 2019 general elections. The day Lutyens went to vote. Having turned 18 just 2 years ago, I had made it a mandate on myself to vote these elections. I first couldn’t get myself on the electoral roll as I’m a migrant in my current constituency. Then when I got my name on the list, on election day even after a half an hour attempt at finding my election booth, I just couldn’t.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/960/0*cuojUlp8Say8L8uI.jpg" /></figure><p>Let’s start 3 months ago. Around February and March, at this point, the ECI is yet to release official dates and India is at the verge of going to war with Pakistan. Having turned 18 in New Delhi and given it has been a domicile of residence for the last 2 years, by constitution I have my vote in Delhi. Ofc, I don’t have the 10000 impossible documents you need to prove it. I applied through the voter’s helpline app. My application was rejected before BLO verification.</p><p>The next month during a briefing to a BBC world interview, I told the producer and the host about my inability to get a voters registration. The producer contacted the election commission through special channels and the next time I applied my verification was skipped and my name was added to the electoral rolls of Model Town constituency that votes the Member of parliament from Chandini Chowk. I fundamentally disagree on how this happened as well, the very fact that a normal Indian citizen would require contacts or higher placed sources to exercise the universal suffrage one has shown proof to a democratic system that systematically removes voters from it rungs.</p><p>Yet, having my name added to the list was of no great help. The next systematically flawed voter ID card distribution system couldn’t deliver my card before the elections even after having my name added 3 weeks prior to the poll dates. Now, there’s a provision for this as well. You can show your slip and then show proof of identification and you can vote. The Aadhar card is an acceptable form of identification. UIDAI a couple of years ago stopped issuing physical cards and held that any print out is as valid as the card. It’s a paid add-on service to get a printed card today. Yet a print out is not acceptable identification. This shows a system that just doesn’t communicate with other parts of it. How can I get ‘the original Aadhar’ if the issuing organization has made it an optional paid service?</p><p>Skip all of this, I still don’t know my polling booth. It was MC Primary DTU colony. There were 3 MC Primaries on one road. All three polling booths told me it’s okay you cannot vote with this Aadhar anyway. Why do I have to go to a specific polling booth to register my vote? This helps parties like the BJP to win a booth at a time and win elections hence. I should be able at least vote at any booth in my constituency. This can be done with current EVMs as the candidates are the same. On a larger scale, Just take this example, some who lives in North Delhi and works in Gurgaon. Corporate offices don’t have a holiday for elections. Even if the person makes 2 hours of time to vote and leaves at 4, the system is literally asking to go to a location probably very far away just to vote. People just don’t vote as a result. India is no more a fully state-run economy. A national holiday doesn’t really mean a holiday for most of India’s workforce today. An election day is not a public holiday for all the countries who run their call centres in India. No outsourcing country has a holiday for Indian election days. Also, why are we allocating voters in printed paper even now?</p><p>How people solve this ‘nuisance’ is by choosing to just not vote. Just 60% of Delhi voted. Last time when BJP swept Delhi they had a 46.40% vote share percentage. Which means 27.96% of the people who live in Delhi elected their representatives to parliament. Now, over the course of this election season, I have asked a lot of people if they have a vote or plan on voting. Every rickshaw wala I asked or even every uber driver I asked just had one single answer, hum ko vote Nahi ye idhar (we don’t have a vote). Delhi has a population of 16,787,941 (<a href="https://www.census2011.co.in/census/state/delhi.html)and">www.census2011.co.in/census/state/delhi.html) and</a> in 2014 it had 8,271,766 voters.</p><p>The disparity in these numbers are stark and would have only increased in the last 8 years. The economic times called Delhi, the Mini India representsIndiainamicrocosm(Read more at:economictimes.indiatimes.com/articleshow)</p><p>If Delhi is a Microcosm of India, then this is really bad. The solutions to these are not even in a debate is what is the saddest part here. To give due credit, India has held on to democracy ever since Independence. The true spirits of it were shown when the only time the spirits of democracy where structurally questioned it resulted in the shameless removal of a leader with Charishma 10 times more than anything Narendra Modi could ever have. A leader who even after attacking her father, Modi wouldn’t dare to touch. The same electorate bought her back when they felt her alternative was miles worse. It was also a display of Indian Federalism. After being disqualified and later thrown out of power in North India, the Congress and Indira Gandhi fought back and won back the crown from the southern and northeastern parts of India. Indira Gandhi from Medak would be the closest South India would have gotten to getting a relevant Prime Minister. The North East got that through Manmohan Singh years later. I’m sorry Kashmir, You got the first.</p><p>It’s clear as a lot of other things, Indians are good at using systems. We have proven that we will keep a democracy regardless. Just like in a lot of sectors, Indians are really bad at making necessary changes when required. Honestly, Indira Gandhi in her own ways fulfilled her Idealist fathers visions for a post-Independent India. Socialist benefits were at their best. International relations at their best. The problem that started haunting her and multiple congress governments after was that India was growing. In all numeric values. It had to adapt. Sadly Indira Gandhi’s response was to cut down on population growth rather than scaling up for the growing population. Indira Gandhi wouldn’t have known that Data would one day become the new oil and numbers would help.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*F4lbjHlCmWXCGdn3DjFMZg.png" /><figcaption>Indian Population has compounded 278%. What was designed in 1951 just CANNOT work in 2019!</figcaption></figure><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/333/0*VHx8FHrCVVbSzMM0.JPG" /><figcaption>This is probably the most graceful Indian International Relations photo of all times.</figcaption></figure><p>Further, it’s probably The Indira Gandhi Wave and it’s apparent ‘undercurrents’ that lead multiple congress governments into power. Even today they hope a similar looking figure to her might help them.</p><p>I would not say the Congress ran the country down. Coz The Indian National Congress is an organisation founded in 1885 by a colonist. It was an idealist socialist party. They did the 1991 reforms. So all political parties are a collection of individuals. Even the BJP keeps evolving. The BJP under Atal Bihari Vajpayee is not the same as the one under Narendra Modi.</p><p>India is a parliamentary democracy. You only elect your local representatives. Then India is a Union of states. Which means that there is proportional representation. So if your local MP or MLA is someone who you agree with then please vote for them.</p><p>The Aam Aadmi Party coming to power crushing the Congress in the National Capital would be the next to test to democracy India passed. This also proved the value of every single representative than one single hero.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/630/0*cJfRSOn0I6zaR310.jpg" /></figure><p>That is the beauty of the Indian Election. You can be a criminal, you can be gay, you can eat beef, you can watch porn and if you’re an Indian Citizen above 18 you have a vote. This is called Universal Adult Franchise in British parliamentary language.</p><p>The only person who can fix these problems is the Election Commission of India. ECI is a Wonderwall. Probably its the autonomy it holds that has held India’s Democracy together. Yet, just like most Indian Public Administrative bodies, the ECI lacks innovation and adaption.</p><p>The Following would be Ideal changes into Indian voting systems to adapt the process for the new Indian Voter. The new Indian voter does not live in one place for the entirety of his life or cares enough to spend hours to just vote.</p><ol><li>Make new EVM’s: Dear ECI, Your EVMs are great. No one can prove they are temperable. Just think about it this way. EVM is some form of computer? It currently has 3 units. Today A smartphone is more powerful and secure than that. A smartphone in all honesty stores information more sensitive than election numbers. Yet, even a smartphone is hackable. The number of technological innovations that are happening in multiple fields related to electronic voting has just been Ignored. Develop a blockchain based system. Andra Pradesh did that for land claims for the Development of its new state capital. Regardless of how expensive the system gets, spend that money. Borrow it, there’ll be a million donors to fund bettering a democracy.</li><li>If scaled to currently developed technology and that’s the thing, everything is ready made. All the required technology is already developed by companies like Jio, Airtel, Google, Microsoft and Amazon. With this, anyone can vote from any point for any constituency. Given we already have an extensive Aadhar network, just place your fingerprint and vote. Simple.</li><li>Then, once these changes are made, reduce election time. Conduct nearby areas together. The only low-income worker with a vote I saw was a carpenter from Ghaziabad who comes every day at 8 to work in North Delhi. Ghaziabad is NCR but is a UP constituency. Ghaziabad elections were multiple weeks prior to Delhi elections. Delhi had a holiday for Delhi Elections not for UP elections also.</li></ol><p>These changes would first bring back trust in the system, with the number of hiccups these machines had this election season it is impossible to believe that they are not ever tamperable. Even if it is untamperable, change it as the world, India and technology have changed.</p><p>As the ease of voting also increases, more people would go to polls. Also if this gets to a point where anyone can vote during any phase of the election. The larger cult of India’s Non Residents can also make an attempt at participating. In 2014,814.5 million people were eligible to vote, with an increase of 100 million voters since the last general election in 2009, making it the largest ever election in the world. Around 23.1 million or 2.7% of the total eligible voters were aged 18–19 years. 553,801,801 people voted. India’s population is 1,366,657,729. Out of all the votes cast BJP had a majority of 31% ie. ‭17,16,78,558 votes. 12.56% of India’s current population voted Modi in.</p><p>The people in India who are being badly affected by the repercussions of everything bad happening aren’t voting. If it’s the English speaking elite of Lutyens that decide the leaders of this nation we are no more a democracy. Ofc, only established people who have the privilege to have a permanent address should vote. At least the Maharaja’s of India were not greedy.</p><p>These are not measures to beat the BJP. Just like one of the Rikshawalas I spoke to told me Modi hi bapas ayega, woh aadmi badiya hai. Modi can and should come back to power but that should be at the mandate of a majority of this country and not the majority of election polling booths.</p><p>Secret insider information public information, every party spends almost 60 crores a booth if they have to win it.</p><p>Modi Badiya hai! but Please vote.</p><img src="https://medium.com/_/stat?event=post.clientViewed&referrerSource=full_rss&postId=38346e230352" width="1" height="1" alt="">
